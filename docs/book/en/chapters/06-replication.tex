\chapter{Replication, ISR, and High Watermarks}
This chapter moves from a single-machine log to replication. \textbf{First, read the boundaries of the course's distributed model carefully:} three independent brokers each have a TCP server, threads, and a disk directory; a follower must fetch records over TCP, and cannot replicate by sharing memory. However, they run in the same JVM and are managed by one trusted ClusterAuthority. The authority linearizes metadata, epoch, and HW state, making teaching failovers controllable. It is not KRaft, has no controller consensus or authority HA, and makes no guarantee about cluster recovery after loss of the entire JVM/authority.

Replication is meaningful only when append, reporting, and visibility boundaries are consistent with one another. LEO is the next offset in each individual log. ISR is the set of replicas currently allowed to participate in acknowledgments. HW is the committed boundary readable by ordinary consumers; consumers can read only offsets < HW. Replicas fetch over a real network; the authority provides consistent state and role decisions, but does not move data.

\lessonsection{21}{Fetch-Based Replication}
\goals{Have followers persist the leader's records locally through \pathcode{REPLICA_FETCH} requests.}{Understand leader LEO, follower LEO, and why a replica fetch differs from a consumer fetch.}
\background{In the first state-monitor critical section, FollowerReplicator verifies that it is online and a follower and captures the epoch; it then performs \pathcode{REPLICA_FETCH} entirely outside the lock. The leader returns records, epoch, HW, and leader LEO; replication can read up to the current leader LEO and is not restricted by the ordinary-consumer HW. The follower checks the response epoch, first offset, continuity, and alignment with the append offset, then enters a second short critical section, rechecks the epoch, rechecks that it is online and a follower, and performs either an empty poll or a log append within that same critical section. The provided ReplicaState.update publishes the role synchronously, preventing a role switch between the final check and append; the network call does not hold the state monitor. Only after the local append is persisted can the next poll report progress. Step 21's network path reads and writes logs only; it does not call the not-yet-implemented report.}
\providededit{The three-node TCP harness, \pathcode{REPLICA_FETCH} message type, ReplicaState, and PartitionLog lifecycle are provided; the fixture seed places the leader's actual LEO in the initial progress.}{\pathcode{exercises/src/main/java/io/simplekafka/replication/FollowerReplicator.java}: \method{pollOnce(int,int)}; also add the \pathcode{REPLICA_FETCH} branch to BrokerHandler.}
\lessoncontract{Only the current online follower can replicate the correct partition. Capture the epoch before sending the request; after receiving the response, validate its format outside the network lock, then recheck the current epoch and role inside the local state monitor. An epoch change in the meantime reports \method{FENCED_EPOCH}; a role change to leader/offline reports \method{NOT_LEADER}; neither failure may append. The broker conservatively caps oversized positive \pathcode{maxBytes} to a single-frame reply budget; bounded batches preserve contiguous follower progress across polls. Repeated empty polls do not rewrite data. Step 21 verifies replica data consistency only; it does not report progress or advance HW.}
\lessonhints{Observe “network response received” and “local persistence completed” as two separate phases.}{The response contains several boundary values; understand the different purposes of HW and leader LEO.}{Poll the follower repeatedly, provide an incorrect response epoch, and provide a mismatched first offset; confirm that only a valid batch changes the local LEO.}
\expected{The leader contains offsets 0–2 and the follower starts at 0; after one poll, reading the follower's disk returns the same key/value pairs and offsets 0, 1, and 2. A second empty poll returns 0 records and leaves LEO unchanged. An incorrect epoch cannot change the follower. Large replies are paged within the frame budget and copied in successive polls. See the real TCP and file assertions in \method{Step21Test}.}
\pitfalls{Sharing the leader's PartitionLog object directly bypasses TCP and local persistence, so the test no longer evaluates replication. Treating HW as the leader LEO prevents a follower from catching up; treating received bytes as success ignores local write failures. Real Kafka has batched replication requests and more epoch/state interactions; the course's custom \pathcode{REPLICA_FETCH} is a teaching API.}
\lessoncommand{21}
\lessonquestions{Why can a follower replicate records beyond the visibility boundary for ordinary consumers?}{Why must the follower not report a new LEO before its local append completes?}

\lessonsection{22}{ISR and High Watermark}
\goals{Maintain ISR and HW from replica progress reports, and remove replicas that lag for too long under a controlled clock.}{Understand minISR, monotonic LEOs, and the high-watermark formula.}
\background{Each replica's progress is protected by a per-partition lock. Only an assigned/online replica with a valid current epoch may report a nonnegative LEO. A report from the leader advances leaderLEO; a follower's report must not exceed the leader's current LEO and must not move backward within the same epoch. A follower refreshes its caught-up time only when it reaches the leader LEO observed at that time; being connected and online alone does not prove synchronization. An expired replica is removed from ISR, but the leader is not. If ISR contains the leader and has at least minISR members, the candidate HW is the minimum of the most recent successful LEO reports in ISR; HW can only advance monotonically. If the minISR condition is not met, HW freezes. Real replication polls are connected to reporting and HW advancement starting in Step 22.}
\providededit{ClusterAuthority provides stable per-TopicPartition locks/Conditions, online status, and progress containers; TimeSource is controllable.}{\pathcode{exercises/src/main/java/io/simplekafka/replication/ReplicationTracker.java}: \method{report(int,int,long)}, \method{expireLagging()}, \method{advanceHighWatermark()}.}
\lessoncontract{Accept reports only from assigned/online replicas at the current epoch with nonnegative LEO; an invalid report must not modify state. A follower's LEO must be <= leaderLEO and must not move backward within the same epoch. If minISR is not met, HW freezes; otherwise HW=max(oldHW,min(ISR progress)). Wake waiters when report or expiration changes a visible condition. The injected clock defines the time boundary, and the leader is not an expiring replica.}
\lessonhints{First list the two boundaries each follower report must satisfy: “not greater than” and “not moving backward.”}{Calculate candidate HW with both sufficient and insufficient ISR, noting that the high watermark cannot move backward.}{For one set of progress values, observe a catch-up, one expiration, and a minISR decrease, including reports, ISR, HW, and waiter wake-ups.}
\expected{With leader/follower LEO=(5,3,4) and minISR=2, HW=3; after one follower reports 5, HW=5. If only the leader remains and it appends through 6, minISR=2 is not satisfied and HW remains at 5. A follower whose lag exceeds the lag timeout is removed from ISR; the leader is not. See the controllable-clock checks in \method{Step22Test}.}
\pitfalls{Treating every online replica as ISR counts lagging replicas toward acknowledgment; calculating HW from a local LEO that has not been successfully reported can expose unverified data to readers. This course's authority retains the committed prefix and requires a clean candidate not to fall below the known HW during a controlled failover; that is an additional teaching assumption. Real Kafka does not guarantee the global monotonicity across epochs described here.}
\lessoncommand{22}
\lessonquestions{Why can appended data not advance HW when minISR is not met?}{Why must a follower that is online but has not caught up to leaderLEO not refresh its caught-up time?}

\lessonsection{23}{Produce Acknowledgments}
\goals{Connect producer acknowledgment semantics to replication state and deadlines, distinguishing “leader has written” from “replicas have committed.”}{Understand Condition waiting, epoch fencing, and timeouts that do not roll back appends.}
\background{ACKS supports only LEADER=1 and ALL=-1. LEADER can return after the leader log append completes, although trailing data may be lost during a failover. ALL must first verify ISR>=minISR, then append and wait for HW to reach batch.nextOffset; while waiting, minISR may decrease or the leader epoch may change. The Condition belongs to the authority partition lock; awaitNanos releases the lock so replication reports can proceed, and on waking the code rechecks epoch, ISR, HW, and deadline. The deadline uses the monotonic clock System.nanoTime; a timeout does not roll back an append. All socket network calls are outside the partition lock.}
\providededit{ReplicationTracker is bound to the authority condition and provides snapshots/locks; RecordData, AppendResult, Acks, and TimeSource are provided.}{\pathcode{exercises/src/main/java/io/simplekafka/replication/AckPolicy.java}: \method{validateBeforeAppend(Acks)}, \method{await(AppendResult,Acks,int,long)}.}
\lessoncontract{LEADER may return as soon as the append completes. Before append, ALL reports \method{NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS} if ISR is insufficient; after append, it waits for HW>=nextOffset. If the epoch changes while waiting, report \method{FENCED_EPOCH}; if ISR falls below minISR, report \method{NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS}; if the deadline expires, report \method{REQUEST_TIMEOUT}. A timeout does not roll back the written log, and the deadline duration must be nonnegative.}
\lessonhints{Distinguish the feasibility check before append from the wait condition after append; they occur at different times and have different errors.}{A Condition wake-up only indicates that “the state may have changed”; it does not mean the condition has been satisfied.}{Use latches to control the order of replica reports: make an ALL request wait first, then unblock it with a report; separately verify the difference between LEO and HW after a timeout.}
\expected{An ALL request remains incomplete after append while a follower has not reported; it succeeds once a report advances HW to nextOffset. A wait timeout leaves the leader LEO advanced, but consumers still cannot see records above HW; a LEADER request can succeed while its record is not yet visible. See the thread-coordination test in \method{Step23Test}.}
\pitfalls{Waiting for replication while holding the lock can block the only progress entry point and cause deadlock; resending a timed-out request can create duplicate records; using a wall clock for deadlines is affected by clock adjustments. Real Kafka has more complex acks/ISR configuration and request queues; the course supports only two ACK modes and does not retry automatically.}
\lessoncommand{23}
\lessonquestions{Why must epoch and ISR be checked again after awaitNanos releases the lock?}{After an ALL timeout, what is the relationship among LEO, HW, and whether the caller knows an append occurred?}

\lessonsection{24}{Concurrent Replicated Broker}
\goals{Combine role checks, appends, replication acknowledgments, and HW-truncated fetches in a broker that serves requests concurrently.}{Understand lock ordering, waiting across sockets, and waking waiters on close.}
\background{ReplicatedPartition connects authority state to local logs as a business operation. Under the authority partition lock, produce verifies leader/epoch, appends, updates its own progress, and advances HW; while ALL is waiting, it releases the partition lock so another TCP worker can report follower progress. fetch can read only the complete prefix below HW: an offset >=HW within the valid range returns empty, while an offset beyond LEO is out of range. Network fetches for replication must also run outside the lock; do not wait on another broker's socket while holding the authority lock. Lock order is fixed as authority lock→local log lock; do not acquire locks in the reverse order or nest locks for different partitions. Closing sockets/schedulers must wake ACK waiters and complete them with an error.}
\providededit{The replicated broker's independent endpoints, broker workers, ReplicaState, ReplicationTracker, AckPolicy, and PartitionLog are provided; the single-machine handler continues to be reused.}{\pathcode{exercises/src/main/java/io/simplekafka/replication/ReplicatedPartition.java}: \method{produce(List<RecordData>,Acks,int,long)}, \method{fetch(long,int,int,int)}; BrokerHandler explicitly routes to the replicated backend.}
\lessoncontract{produce preserves the linearized order “role and epoch validation → pre-append ACK check → log append → leader report/HW update → acknowledgment wait”; it must not hold the partition lock across network calls. fetch returns records at offsets <HW and truncates to the total budget; an offset in [logStart,LEO] that is also >=HW returns empty, while offset>LEO is out of range. An epoch mismatch is fenced. The single-machine and replicated backends are selected explicitly, without falling back by catching an exception.}
\lessonhints{Draw the locks acquired by the produce, follower-poll, and fetch threads, marking the network boundaries.}{Check that the HW limit takes effect in fetch before the broker returns results to an ordinary consumer.}{Pause one follower's replication, keep another socket fetching, and let an ALL produce wait; then resume replication and observe that all three operations can make progress.}
\expected{Pause follower replication; while ALL produce waits, a fetch over another connection can see only the committed prefix. After replication resumes and reports progress, produce succeeds and the new batch becomes visible. When the broker closes, pending requests complete with errors and the JVM has no worker left blocked. See the real TCP concurrency acceptance test in \method{Step24Test}.}
\pitfalls{Holding a lock across a network call can prevent the replication thread from acquiring it, and a single-threaded test may not expose this. Sharing HW with the committed group offset confuses replication visibility with consumer progress. Real Kafka runs multiple processes and includes complex controller behavior during network partitions; the independent networks/disks here still share one authority in the same JVM and do not demonstrate production HA.}
\lessoncommand{24}
\lessonquestions{Why can another fetch safely read the old HW prefix concurrently while ALL is waiting?}{When closing, is closing only the socket enough to guarantee that threads waiting on a Condition will finish?}
